## Safety Information

[Follow general safety practices](/general-information/saftey/general-safety.md)

### Known Risks

- Bacterial contamination if kernels are over-hydrated
- Mold contamination if sterilization fails
- Burns from pressure cooker and boiling water
- Respiratory spore exposure during fruiting

### Containment Requirements

- Pressure cooker capable of 15 PSI required
- Still Air Box (SAB) or Laminar Flow Hood for inoculation
- Clean, low-traffic workspace

### Safety Equipment

- Nitrile gloves
- N95 respirator
- Heat-resistant gloves for pressure cooker
- 70% isopropyl alcohol

### Emergency Procedures

- Contaminated jars: seal in plastic bag immediately, remove from grow space
- Steam burns: cool water for 10+ minutes, seek medical attention for severe burns

### Storage Safety

- Colonized popcorn spawn stores 4-6 weeks at 2-4°C (35-40°F)
- Label all containers with species, strain, and inoculation date

### Disposal Requirements

- Contaminated grain: seal and dispose in garbage
- Spent spawn: compost-safe after full exhaustion

## Required Materials

### Essential Materials

- Plain, un-popped popcorn kernels (yellow or white, NO butter, NO salt, NO additives)
- Wide-mouth quart or pint mason jars
- Pressure cooker capable of 15 PSI
- Jar lids with polyfill filter patches and optional injection ports
- 70% isopropyl alcohol
- Still Air Box (SAB)
- Alcohol lamp, torch, or lighter
- Spore syringe or liquid culture
- Tin foil

### Optional Materials

- Gypsum (calcium sulfate) — 1 tablespoon per quart jar to prevent clumping
- Injection port lids for reduced contamination risk during inoculation
- Flow hood for improved inoculation environment

### Recommended Suppliers

- Plain popcorn: Grocery stores, bulk bins (as of 2026-MAR: ~$0.80-1.50/lb)
- Mason jars: Hardware stores, Walmart (as of 2026-MAR: ~$1.00-2.00/jar)

## Protocol

### Step 1: Kernel Preparation

**Trigger/Timing:** Day before or day of sterilization

#### Procedure:

1. Measure and rinse kernels:
   - Use plain un-popped popcorn — no additives whatsoever
   - Rinse 2-3 times in cold water, discarding any floating kernels or debris

2. Simmer kernels:
   - Add rinsed kernels to pot with 3x volume of water
   - Bring to boil, reduce to simmer
   - Simmer for 25-35 minutes (popcorn kernels are denser than rye/WBS)
   - Kernels should be plump and pliable — a slight star-crack at the germ end is normal and acceptable
   - Kernels should NOT be fully split, exploded (popped), or mushy

3. Drain and surface-dry:
   - Drain kernels through colander
   - Spread on clean towels or baking sheets
   - Fan-dry for 30-60 minutes until matte (not wet/shiny)
   - Kernels should not stick together when squeezed

**Quality Indicators:**

- Kernels plump but intact (slight germ crack acceptable)
- No split or popped kernels
- Surface matte — no visible surface moisture
- Kernels separate freely

**Control Points:**

- Over-hydration is the primary failure point for popcorn TEK — prefer slightly under-done over over-done
- Remove any fully popped or split kernels before loading jars

**Troubleshooting:**

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
| ------- | ----- | -------- |
| Many kernels popped | Temperature too high | Use lower simmer; test at 20 minutes |
| Kernels still rock-hard | Under-cooked | Extend simmer time; test every 5 minutes |

### Step 2: Jar Loading

**Trigger/Timing:** After kernels have surface-dried

#### Procedure:

1. Prepare lids with filter patches and optional injection ports

2. Optionally mix in gypsum:
   - 1 tablespoon gypsum per dry quart of popcorn
   - Mix well before loading jars

3. Fill jars:
   - Fill to 50-60% capacity
   - Leave ample headspace
   - Tap to settle; do not pack
   - Wipe jar rim clean before lidding

4. Cover with foil for sterilization

**Quality Indicators:**

- Jars 50-60% full
- Clean rims and secure lids
- Filter patches intact

### Step 3: Sterilization

**Trigger/Timing:** Immediately after jar loading

#### Procedure:

1. Load pressure cooker with rack and water

2. Arrange jars on rack above waterline

3. Sterilization cycle:
   - Vent steam 10 minutes before applying weight
   - Bring to 15 PSI
   - Maintain **90-120 minutes** at 15 PSI

4. Cool down:
   - Natural pressure drop
   - Rest 12-24 hours before inoculation

**Growth Parameters:**

- Temperature: 121°C (250°F) at 15 PSI
- Time: 90-120 minutes

**Quality Indicators:**

- Full sterilization time achieved
- No contamination after cooling
- Kernels remain intact and structured

**Control Points:**

- Popcorn has hard outer hull that resists heat penetration — do not reduce sterilization time below 90 minutes
- Full cooling essential before inoculation

### Step 4: Inoculation

**Trigger/Timing:** After 12-24 hours cooling

#### Procedure:

1. Set up SAB and wipe with isopropyl alcohol
2. Flame-sterilize and cool needle
3. Inject 1-3 mL per pint / 2-4 mL per quart jar
4. Inject through polyfill or injection port
5. Re-sterilize needle every 2-3 jars
6. Remove foil covers after inoculation

**Quality Indicators:**

- Sterile technique maintained
- Appropriate injection volume
- No air currents during procedure

### Step 5: Colonization

**Trigger/Timing:** After inoculation — store in warm, dark area

#### Procedure:

1. Store jars in 24-28°C (75-82°F) dark area

2. Monitor daily for growth and contamination

3. Shake at 20-30% colonization to distribute mycelium

4. Wait for full colonization (2-4 weeks) plus 5-7 days consolidation

**Growth Parameters:**

- Temperature: 24-28°C (75-82°F)
- CO₂: Elevated during colonization
- Light: None required
- Time: 2-4 weeks to full colonization

**Quality Indicators:**

- Dense white mycelium throughout jar
- No contamination visible
- Earthy mushroomy smell
- Grain fused into cohesive mycelial mass when ready

**Control Points:**

- Temperature stability critical
- Discard contaminated jars immediately
- Do not open jars during colonization

**Troubleshooting:**

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
| ------- | ----- | -------- |
| Green mold | Contamination during inoculation | Improve SAB/sterile technique |
| Wet rot (black, sour) | Over-hydrated kernels | Dry kernels more thoroughly |
| No growth in 2 weeks | Non-viable inoculant or low temperature | Test inoculant; raise temperature |

## Optimizations

### Why Popcorn TEK is Popular

- Popcorn kernels have a hard outer hull that resists contamination better than other grains
- Very low cost and universally available
- Colonizes cleanly with good mycelial density
- Kernels don't clump as severely as rye berries

### Efficiency Improvements

- Use liquid culture instead of spore syringe for 30-50% faster colonization
- Grain-to-grain (G2G) transfer: one colonized jar can inoculate 5-8 new jars
- Pre-soak for 24 hours (cold water) instead of boiling for softer kernels with less risk of popping

### Scaling Considerations

- Scales easily from pint to quart to half-gallon jars
- For commercial scale, transition to autoclave bags with filter patches

## Community Notes

- Popcorn's hard hull makes it more resistant to contamination during preparation than rye or WBS
- The hulls also protect grain during sterilization — less risk of exploding kernels in PC vs. wheat
- Some cultivators prefer adding a small amount of activated charcoal to the PC water to absorb terpenes
- Popcorn spawn has excellent water retention and transfers well to bulk substrates
- A common beginner mistake is using microwave or flavored popcorn — always use plain, raw, un-popped kernels
