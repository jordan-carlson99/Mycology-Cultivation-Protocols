## Safety Information

[Follow general safety practices](/general-information/saftey/general-safety.md)

### Known Risks

- Respiratory exposure to coco coir dust during preparation
- Mold contamination if substrate is insufficiently pasteurized or over-hydrated
- Cross-contamination from spawn introduction
- Spore exposure during fruiting and harvest

### Containment Requirements

- CVG is a pasteurized (not sterilized) substrate — contamination resistance is inherent in the process
- A clean workspace is sufficient; SAB not required for substrate preparation
- SAB or clean environment recommended for spawn introduction

### Safety Equipment

- N95 respirator when mixing dry coco coir (fine dust)
- Nitrile gloves
- 70% isopropyl alcohol for surface sanitation

### Emergency Procedures

- Contaminated substrate: seal in plastic bag and dispose of; do not attempt to salvage
- Respiratory irritation from coco coir dust: move to fresh air; wear respirator for future work

### Storage Safety

- Prepared (pasteurized and hydrated) CVG substrate should be used within 24-48 hours of preparation
- Do not store wet CVG substrate for extended periods — contamination risk increases significantly
- Dry coco coir bricks can be stored indefinitely in cool, dry location

### Disposal Requirements

- Spent CVG substrate is fully compostable
- Contaminated substrate: bag and dispose; heat-treat if composting
- CVG is an environmentally neutral substrate — no hazardous materials

## Required Materials

### Essential Materials

- **Coco Coir**: Coconut coir bricks (compressed, available at garden centers and hydroponic shops)
- **Vermiculite**: Horticultural grade, medium or coarse (NOT fire-rated or construction grade)
- **Gypsum** (calcium sulfate): Garden gypsum, food grade preferred
- Boiling water (for pasteurization)
- Large mixing bowl or bucket
- Monotub or fruiting container
- Thermometer
- Grain spawn (colonized — see WBS TEK, Grain Spawn TEK, or Popcorn TEK)
- 70% isopropyl alcohol (for surface sanitation)

### Optional Materials

- pH meter (target 6.0-7.0 for CVG)
- Kitchen scale for precise measurements
- Parchment paper or aluminum foil (for covering during pasteurization)
- Polyfill or filter patch covers for monotub lids

### Recommended Suppliers

- Coco coir bricks: Home Depot, Lowe's, hydroponic stores, Amazon (as of 2026-MAR: ~$5-15/brick, expands to ~8-10 liters)
- Vermiculite: Home Depot, garden centers (as of 2026-MAR: ~$10-20/4 qt bag)
- Gypsum: Garden centers, hydroponic stores (as of 2026-MAR: ~$5-10/bag)

## Protocol

### Step 1: Substrate Mixing

**Trigger/Timing:** When colonized grain spawn is ready

#### Procedure:

**Standard CVG Recipe (per quart of dry coco coir):**

| Component | Volume | Notes |
| --------- | ------ | ----- |
| Coco coir | 1 block (~650g compressed) | Expands to ~8-10 liters |
| Vermiculite | Equal volume to expanded coco coir | By volume, not weight |
| Gypsum | 1-2 tablespoons | Per liter of substrate mix |
| Boiling water | As needed for field capacity | ~1 liter per compressed brick |

1. Prepare substrate mixture:
   - Place coco coir brick in large bucket or mixing bowl
   - Pour boiling water over coco coir — approximately 1 liter per compressed brick
   - Allow coco coir to absorb water and expand (5-10 minutes)
   - Break up coco coir chunks with hands or spatula until uniform

2. Add vermiculite:
   - Add vermiculite in equal volume to expanded coco coir (1:1 ratio by volume)
   - Mix thoroughly

3. Add gypsum:
   - Add 1-2 tablespoons of gypsum per liter of substrate
   - Gypsum improves substrate structure and provides supplemental calcium

4. Check field capacity:
   - Squeeze a handful of substrate firmly
   - At field capacity: only a few drops of water come out
   - Too wet: excess water streams out — add more dry vermiculite to adjust
   - Too dry: no water comes out — add small amounts of warm water and recheck

5. Allow to cool:
   - Cover substrate with foil or clean lid
   - Allow to cool to room temperature (below 30°C / 86°F) before adding spawn
   - Cooling also allows pasteurization to complete
   - This takes approximately 2-4 hours

**Quality Indicators:**

- Substrate is uniformly mixed with even moisture distribution
- Squeezing produces only a few drops (field capacity)
- No large dry clumps or overly wet patches
- Substrate temperature below 30°C (86°F) before spawning

**Control Points:**

- Boiling water pasteurizes the coco coir — the substrate should reach 65-80°C (149-176°F) internally
- Coco coir has natural anti-contaminant properties (low pH, tannins), making CVG very resistant to contamination
- Field capacity moisture is critical — over-wet substrate promotes bacterial contamination

**Troubleshooting:**

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
| ------- | ----- | -------- |
| Substrate too wet after mixing | Too much water added | Mix in additional dry vermiculite |
| Substrate too dry | Insufficient water | Add warm water in small increments, recheck FC |
| Coco coir not expanding | Insufficient water or insufficient time | Add more boiling water; allow 15 minutes |
| Lumpy, uneven texture | Insufficient mixing | Break up by hand and mix thoroughly |

### Step 2: Spawning to Bulk

**Trigger/Timing:** After substrate has cooled to room temperature AND grain spawn is fully colonized

#### Procedure:

**Spawn Rate:** 10-20% by volume (e.g., 1 quart spawn to 5 quarts CVG)

**Method A: Layer Method (Reduces contamination risk)**

1. Wipe hands and all surfaces with isopropyl alcohol
2. Add a layer of CVG substrate to bottom of monotub (approximately 1 inch)
3. Break up grain spawn into individual kernels
4. Spread a layer of grain spawn over CVG layer
5. Add another layer of CVG on top
6. Optionally repeat for 3-layer structure
7. Top with a casing layer of pure CVG or casing mix (optional)

**Method B: Mixed Method (Faster colonization)**

1. Wipe hands and all surfaces with isopropyl alcohol
2. Combine spawn and substrate in large bowl
3. Mix thoroughly to distribute spawn evenly throughout substrate
4. Transfer mixed substrate to monotub
5. Level surface gently — do not compact

3. Cover and begin colonization:
   - Cover monotub with lid (with or without filter patches)
   - For unmodified lids: leave lid slightly offset for gas exchange, or crack open briefly twice daily
   - Target colonization environment as specified below

**Quality Indicators:**

- Even spawn distribution throughout substrate (mixed method) or evenly layered (layer method)
- Spawn rate approximately 10-20%
- Substrate depth 3-6 inches in monotub for good fruiting performance

**Control Points:**

- Avoid working near HVAC vents or fans when spawning (contamination risk)
- Work quickly — minimize exposure time of substrate and spawn to air
- Monotub should be wiped clean with isopropyl before use

### Step 3: Colonization of Bulk Substrate

**Trigger/Timing:** After spawning

#### Procedure:

1. Store monotub in colonization environment:
   - Dark area with stable temperature
   - Maintain optimal temperature range for species

2. Monitor for colonization progress:
   - White mycelium should begin showing within 5-10 days
   - Full colonization of 3-6 inch substrate layer typically takes 7-14 days
   - Look for white, fluffy to ropy mycelium progressing across the surface and through the substrate

3. Watch for contamination:
   - Green, black, pink mold = contamination (discard affected tubs)
   - Yellow or brown discoloration may indicate metabolite secretion or early contamination

4. Mark as ready for fruiting:
   - When entire substrate surface is covered in white mycelium
   - Pinning may initiate on its own even before FAE begins

**Growth Parameters:**

- Temperature: 24-28°C (75-82°F) for cubensis; adjust per species
- Humidity: Not critical during colonization (monotub lid closed)
- CO₂: Elevated during colonization (lid closed or minimally opened)
- Light: Minimal or none during colonization
- Time: 7-14 days for bulk substrate to colonize from grain spawn

**Quality Indicators:**

- Uniform white mycelial coverage across substrate surface
- No contamination visible
- Substrate may consolidate (compress slightly) as mycelium colonizes
- Metabolites (yellow droplets) may appear on mycelium surface — normal

**Control Points:**

- Consistent temperature is critical — avoid temperature swings
- Do not add water to substrate during colonization
- Minimize tub disturbances during colonization

**Troubleshooting:**

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
| ------- | ----- | -------- |
| Green mold on surface | Trichoderma contamination | Discard tub; improve spawn hygiene |
| No growth after 2 weeks | Low temperature or non-viable spawn | Raise temperature; check spawn viability |
| Patchy or uneven colonization | Uneven spawn distribution | Ensure even mixing during spawning |
| Yellow metabolites on surface | Normal physiological response | Monitor; if mycelium is healthy, proceed normally |

### Step 4: Initiating Fruiting

**Trigger/Timing:** When entire substrate surface is colonized

#### Procedure:

1. Introduce fruiting conditions:
   - Begin Fresh Air Exchanges (FAE) — open/fan tub 2-3 times daily for 30-60 seconds
   - Or use a modified monotub with filter patches (passive FAE)
   - Mist walls of tub (NOT directly on substrate) to raise humidity
   - Reduce temperature by 2-4°C from colonization temperature to initiate pinning

2. Pinning initiation:
   - First primordia (pins) typically appear within 5-10 days of introducing fruiting conditions
   - Pins appear as tiny white dots that develop into recognizable mushroom shapes

3. Pin development:
   - Maintain humidity at 80-95% RH
   - Continue FAE 2-3x daily
   - Mist walls only — do not mist directly on pins
   - Maintain ambient light (12 on/12 off) as signal for fruiting

4. Harvest:
   - Harvest mushrooms just before or as the veil beneath the cap begins to tear
   - Twist and pull, or use clean scissors/knife at substrate level
   - Remove all remaining stump material (prevent contamination sites)

**Growth Parameters:**

- Temperature: 20-24°C (68-75°F) for cubensis; adjust per species
- Humidity: 80-95% RH
- CO₂: <1000 ppm (FAE critical for pin formation)
- Light: 12 hours on / 12 hours off (indirect)
- Time: 5-10 days to first pins; 5-7 additional days to harvest

**Quality Indicators:**

- Multiple pins forming across substrate surface
- Caps developing normally (not elongated, which indicates excess CO₂)
- Veil intact on developing fruit bodies
- Clean, white to light brown caps at harvest

**Control Points:**

- FAE is the most critical factor — insufficient FAE causes elongated, spindly pins or no pins at all
- Humidity must be maintained but condensation on pins can cause blotching
- Harvest timing is important: pre-veil-tear harvests give best yields and prevent spore dispersal

### Step 5: Post-Harvest (Second and Subsequent Flushes)

**Trigger/Timing:** After completing harvest of each flush

#### Procedure:

1. Field cap harvest:
   - Remove all stubs and aborts to prevent contamination
   - Wipe tub walls clean of spore deposits

2. Dunk and roll (optional but recommended between flushes):
   - Remove substrate block from tub (if it holds together)
   - Submerge in clean water for 12-24 hours
   - Return to tub and restart fruiting conditions

3. OR surface rehydrate:
   - Add 1-2 cups of clean water to tub surface
   - Allow to absorb; drain excess after 30 minutes

4. Resume fruiting conditions:
   - Second flush typically appears 7-14 days after rehydration
   - CVG substrate typically supports 2-4 flushes

**Quality Indicators:**

- Substrate surface still mostly white/colonized (minimal contamination)
- Rehydrated substrate at field capacity
- Second flush pins appearing within 2 weeks

**Control Points:**

- Remove all stubs and aborts — these are primary contamination sites between flushes
- Contamination risk increases with each successive flush

## Optimizations

### Efficiency Improvements

- **Higher spawn rate (25-30%)**: Faster colonization, reduced contamination risk, may increase yield on first flush
- **Casing layer**: Adding a thin (1/2 inch) layer of pure CVG or 100% field capacity coir on top of spawned substrate triggers more uniform pinning
- **Cold shock**: Placing fully colonized tub in refrigerator for 12-24 hours before fruiting can trigger aggressive pin sets

### Scaling Considerations

- CVG scales well from small (6-quart) to large (66-quart) monotubs
- Larger tubs require proportionally more spawn and substrate
- Multiple tubs can be staggered for continuous harvest

### Resource Optimization

- Coco coir bricks are very economical — one brick produces 8-10 liters of substrate
- CVG substrate can be rehydrated and reflashed multiple times before exhaustion
- Spent CVG substrate is an excellent garden amendment or composting material

## Alternatives

- **Straw TEK**: Lower-tech option for oyster mushrooms, no coir needed. [See Shroomery straw resources](https://www.shroomery.org/forums/)
- **CVG + Manure**: Adding composted cow manure enhances cubensis yields
- **Pure Coco Coir**: Slightly less contamination resistant than CVG without vermiculite

## Community Notes

- CVG is widely considered the best beginner bulk substrate due to its contamination resistance and availability
- Coco coir's tannins and naturally low pH provide significant protection against common contaminants
- The "dunk and roll" technique between flushes is widely reported to significantly improve total yield
- Some cultivators add hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) to the substrate water for additional contamination resistance
- CVG pairs excellently with WBS, Rye, or Popcorn grain spawn
- The gypsum component is sometimes omitted without significant impact, but many cultivators prefer it for substrate structure
- Commercial coco coir bricks vary in quality — look for fine-grain, OMRI-listed products for best results
