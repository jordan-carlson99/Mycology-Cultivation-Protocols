## Safety Information

[Follow general safety practices](/general-information/saftey/general-safety.md)

### Known Risks

- Significant spore dispersal — Martha tents are open-access and can release large amounts of spores during FAE or harvest
- Mold contamination risk from automated misting (over-misting creates wet environment)
- Electrical hazards from ultrasonic humidifiers, timers, and heaters near water
- Respiratory exposure to spores, especially with multiple fruiting blocks

### Containment Requirements

- Martha Tent should ideally be set up in a dedicated grow room, basement, or area separate from living/sleeping spaces
- HEPA filtration recommended in the grow room to capture spores
- Automated humidifier misting should use clean, filtered water

### Safety Equipment

- N95 or equivalent respirator (strongly recommended when working inside Martha tent during fruiting)
- Nitrile gloves
- Eye protection when harvesting large crops (spore clouds)
- GFCI outlets required for all electrical equipment near water

### Emergency Procedures

- Electrical issue: immediately unplug all equipment; do not attempt repairs while equipment is wet
- Widespread contamination: remove affected blocks; deep-clean and sterilize tent before resuming
- Allergic reaction to spores: leave grow area; seek medical attention if symptoms persist

### Storage Safety

- Keep all electrical equipment (timers, heaters, humidifiers) away from direct water exposure
- Ensure tent is on a stable, level surface that can support the weight of fruiting blocks and water
- Do not leave ultrasonic humidifiers running unattended for long periods without overflow protection

### Disposal Requirements

- Spent substrate blocks: compost or dispose of in sealed bags
- Used perlite: can be sterilized and reused, or disposed of in garbage
- Contaminated materials: bag and seal before disposal

## Required Materials

### Essential Materials

- **Martha Stewart 4-tier Mini Greenhouse Tent**: The original (~$30-50) or equivalent 4-5 shelf wire rack greenhouse tent
- **Ultrasonic Humidifier** (cool mist): With reservoir capacity of at least 1-2 liters; must have a "fog output" fitting for tubing
- **Humidity controller / Inkbird or similar**: Hygrostat that turns humidifier on/off based on target RH (target 85-95%)
- **Humidity sensor/hygrometer**: Digital hygrometer to monitor RH in tent
- **Hosing/tubing**: Flexible plastic tubing to route humidifier output into tent (1/2" ID)
- **FAE fan**: Small USB or AC fan to provide fresh air exchanges
- **Timer**: For controlling fan and light
- **Thermometer/hygrometer combo** (inside tent)
- **Drip tray or perlite tray**: To catch excess water and provide passive evaporation
- 70% isopropyl alcohol
- Colonized fruiting blocks or monotubs (to place inside tent)

- **Inkbird IHC-200 or equivalent humidity controller** (preferred over basic timer for humidity control)
- **Grow light** (6500K LED or fluorescent): With 12/12 timer for fruiting signal
- **Space heater with thermostat**: For temperature control in cold environments
- **Secondary fan inside tent**: For even air distribution
- **Fog diffuser/humidistat**: For directing mist evenly across all shelves

## Protocol

### Step 1: Tent Assembly and Setup

**Trigger/Timing:** Initial setup — performed before any fruiting blocks are introduced

#### Procedure:

1. Assemble Martha tent/mini greenhouse:
   - Follow manufacturer instructions to assemble wire rack shelving
   - Install clear plastic cover/tent over shelving frame
   - Place tent in chosen grow location (basement, closet, spare room)
   - Place tent on tray or waterproof mat to protect floor from humidity/water

2. Install perlite humidity reservoir (optional but recommended):
   - Line bottom tray of tent with 2-4 inches of perlite
   - Hydrate perlite to field capacity (wet but not pooling)
   - Perlite acts as passive humidity buffer and catches dripping water

3. Set up ultrasonic humidifier:
   - Fill humidifier with clean, filtered, or distilled water (tap water causes mineral deposits)
   - Route output tube into tent through a small gap in the plastic cover
   - Position tube to direct mist toward lower/middle portion of tent
   - Connect humidifier to humidity controller (hygrostat)

4. Set up humidity controller:
   - Place sensor probe inside tent at middle shelf level
   - Set target humidity: 85-95% RH
   - Hygrostat will automatically activate humidifier when RH drops below target

5. Set up FAE fan:
   - Position fan to blow fresh air INTO the tent from one side
   - Provide a vent gap on the opposite side for exhaust
   - Connect fan to timer: 1-5 minute bursts every 2-4 hours (or continuously at low speed)
   - FAE must balance humidity — more FAE = lower RH = more misting needed

6. Set up lighting:
   - Install grow light above tent or use ambient room lighting
   - Connect to timer: 12 hours on / 12 hours off
   - Light is a directional cue for fruiting; blocks should be visible to light source

7. Test system before introducing blocks:
   - Run humidifier and FAE for 24 hours
   - Verify humidity controller is cycling humidifier correctly
   - Verify tent reaches and maintains target humidity (85-95% RH)
   - Verify no electrical hazards (water near outlets, damaged cords)

**Quality Indicators:**

- Tent maintains 85-95% RH when humidifier is operational
- FAE fan cycles air without excessive humidity loss
- All electrical connections safe and dry
- Temperature stable at target range for fruiting

**Control Points:**

- Use distilled or filtered water in humidifier — mineral deposits from tap water clog ultrasonic elements
- Ensure GFCI outlets protect all electrical connections
- Test all equipment before introducing fruiting blocks

**Troubleshooting:**

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
| ------- | ----- | -------- |
| Humidity won't reach target | FAE too aggressive or humidifier too small | Reduce FAE frequency; upgrade humidifier |
| Humidity too high (condensation pooling on blocks) | Insufficient FAE | Increase fan duration/frequency |
| Mineral deposits on humidifier | Hard tap water | Use distilled water; clean humidifier regularly |
| Electrical issue | Wet outlet or damaged cord | Unplug immediately; inspect and replace damaged components |

### Step 2: Loading Fruiting Blocks

**Trigger/Timing:** When colonized fruiting blocks or monotubs are ready for fruiting

#### Procedure:

1. Prepare fruiting blocks:
   - Verify blocks are fully colonized and consolidated
   - Remove any outer plastic bags (for sawdust/grain bags) to expose colonized block
   - For monotubs: position tub open-side up in tent

2. Arrange blocks in tent:
   - Place blocks on shelves with adequate spacing (4-6 inches between blocks for airflow)
   - Heavier, larger blocks on lower shelves
   - Ensure each block has access to light, air, and humidity from humidifier

3. Position humidity sensor:
   - Locate sensor at middle shelf level for representative reading
   - Keep sensor away from direct humidifier mist (causes false high readings)

4. Introduce fruiting conditions:
   - Ensure temperature is in fruiting range (lower than colonization temp)
   - Activate FAE timer
   - Activate lighting timer
   - Hygrostat automatically manages humidity

**Quality Indicators:**

- Blocks adequately spaced for airflow
- Humidity sensor positioned correctly
- All systems operational

**Control Points:**

- Do not overcrowd tent — poor airflow causes uneven fruiting and contamination
- Monitor each block individually — different species or strains may have different needs

### Step 3: Fruiting and Monitoring

**Trigger/Timing:** After all systems are operational and blocks are loaded

#### Procedure:

1. Daily checks:
   - Verify humidity is being maintained (check hygrometer)
   - Check temperature
   - Observe blocks for pin development and contamination
   - Inspect humidifier water level; refill as needed (typically every 1-3 days)

2. Pin development:
   - First pins typically appear 5-10 days after introducing fruiting conditions
   - Pins will appear on exposed block surfaces and sometimes on sides/bottom

3. Managing multiple blocks:
   - Different blocks may be at different stages simultaneously
   - Tag each block with species, strain, and load date for tracking
   - Blocks producing different species can coexist if humidity and temperature requirements are similar

4. Misting adjustments:
   - If condensation pools on blocks: reduce humidifier output or increase FAE
   - If blocks look dry: increase humidifier output or check hygrostat calibration

**Growth Parameters:**

- Temperature: 20-24°C (68-75°F) for cubensis; 15-24°C (59-75°F) for oysters; adjust per species
- Humidity: 85-95% RH maintained by hygrostat
- CO₂: < 1000 ppm (FAE ensures adequate fresh air)
- Light: 12h on / 12h off (indirect LED or fluorescent)
- Time: 5-10 days to first pins; 5-7 additional days to harvest

**Quality Indicators:**

- Uniform pin sets across all blocks
- Caps developing normally with proper cap/stem ratio
- No pooling water on block surfaces
- No contamination visible

**Control Points:**

- FAE is critical — the automated system must be cycling properly
- Monitor temperature, especially if environmental temperatures change seasonally
- Harvest timing is key — harvest before veils tear to prevent massive spore release in grow area

**Troubleshooting:**

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
| ------- | ----- | -------- |
| No pins after 2+ weeks | Insufficient FAE, incorrect humidity, or temperature wrong | Verify all parameters; increase FAE |
| Elongated, spindly pins | Excess CO₂ | Increase FAE |
| Pin abort | Humidity drop or temperature spike | Stabilize conditions |
| Contamination spreading between blocks | Cross-contamination via shared air | Remove affected blocks immediately; increase FAE |
| Condensation pooling on blocks | Over-humidification | Reduce humidifier output; increase FAE |

### Step 4: Harvest

**Trigger/Timing:** When veil begins to tear (just before maximum cap size)

#### Procedure:

1. Wear N95 respirator before entering tent during harvest — spore counts are high

2. Harvest each mushroom:
   - Grip at base and twist gently while pulling
   - Or cut at block surface with clean scissors/knife
   - Harvest all mature fruits including small ones to maximize yield

3. Remove aborts and stubs:
   - Clear all unviable pins and stub remnants
   - These are primary contamination entry points

4. Rehydrate blocks between flushes:
   - Submerge individual blocks in clean cold water for 12-24 hours (dunk method)
   - Return to tent and resume fruiting conditions
   - Second flush typically in 7-14 days

5. Rotate and track blocks:
   - Mark harvest date and flush number on each block
   - Remove blocks that show contamination or exhaustion (typically after 3-5 flushes)
   - Introduce new colonized blocks to maintain continuous production

**Quality Indicators:**

- Clean harvest without stub remnants
- Blocks showing healthy white surface after harvest
- No contamination visible after harvest

**Control Points:**

- Stub removal is critical for multi-flush success
- Use separate tools per block if contamination is suspected to prevent cross-contamination
- Monitor for contamination closely after each flush — risk increases with each cycle

## Optimizations

### Efficiency Improvements

- **Hygrostat automation**: Eliminates need for manual misting — humidity is maintained automatically
- **Staggered production**: Load new blocks every 1-2 weeks for continuous harvest through the year
- **Species pairing**: Some cultivators grow compatible species simultaneously (same temperature/humidity range)

### Scaling Considerations

- Standard 4-tier Martha tent holds 8-16 colonized blocks (2-4 per shelf)
- Upgrade to 5-tier tent or full-size grow tent (4'x4', 4'x8') for larger-scale production
- Multiple tents can be daisy-chained on a single humidifier for high-volume growing
- Commercial-scale operations use purpose-built grow rooms with HVAC, CO₂ control, and automated irrigation

### Resource Optimization

- Distilled water in humidifier extends equipment lifespan significantly
- Reusable perlite in reservoir (sterilize between cycles)
- Martha tent can be used indefinitely with basic maintenance (wipe-down between cycles)

## Alternatives

- **SGFC (Shotgun Fruiting Chamber)**: Better for small-scale PF TEK cake fruiting; no automation needed. [SGFC Method](/protocols/fruiting/SGFC-method/SGFC-method.md)
- **Monotub TEK**: Self-contained, no separate fruiting chamber needed. [Monotub TEK](/protocols/fruiting/Monotub%20TEK/Monotub-TEK%5Bbulk-fruiting%5D.md)
- **Flow hood + chamber**: For high-sterility environments, a laminar flow hood replaces SAB

## Community Notes

- The Martha Tent setup is the most popular automated fruiting chamber on Shroomery for intermediate to advanced cultivators
- The name comes from the "Martha Stewart" branded 4-tier mini greenhouse sold at Walmart and Amazon
- Inkbird humidity controllers are the community standard for hygrostat automation
- The key balance is between humidity (for proper fruiting) and FAE (for CO₂ reduction and pin set)
- Using distilled water in the humidifier is strongly recommended — tap water causes mineral deposits and humidifier failure
- Many cultivators add a second small fan inside the tent to ensure even air distribution across all shelves
- Tent-wide contamination events can be prevented by quarantining suspicious blocks immediately
- The Martha Tent setup is easily expandable — simply add more shelving or a larger tent as production needs grow
