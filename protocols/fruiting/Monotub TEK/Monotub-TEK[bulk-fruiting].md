## Safety Information

[Follow general safety practices](/general-information/saftey/general-safety.md)

### Known Risks

- Spore dispersal during fruiting and harvest — can be substantial with mature caps
- Mold contamination once fruiting chamber is opened to FAE
- Respiratory exposure to spores and substrate dust
- Wet environment can support bacterial growth if not managed properly

### Containment Requirements

- Monotub should be used in an area where spore dispersal is acceptable (NOT shared living/sleeping spaces without proper filtration)
- Keep monotub away from air returns and HVAC systems
- Harvest before veil tears to minimize spore dispersal

### Safety Equipment

- N95 or equivalent respirator during harvesting and substrate preparation
- Nitrile gloves
- 70% isopropyl alcohol for surface sanitation

### Emergency Procedures

- Contaminated monotub: seal in large garbage bag; remove from grow space
- Allergic reaction to spores: leave area, move to fresh air; seek medical attention if symptoms persist

### Storage Safety

- Do not store monotubs in food preparation areas
- Keep out of reach of children and pets
- Label tubs with species, strain, and spawn date

### Disposal Requirements

- Spent substrate: fully composted CVG is an excellent garden amendment
- Contaminated substrate: seal and dispose; heat-treat if composting
- Tubs can be cleaned and reused (sterilize with bleach solution between uses)

## Required Materials

### Essential Materials

- **Monotub**: Clear or translucent storage tub (54-66 quart Sterilite or Rubbermaid tubs most popular)
- **Colonized grain spawn**: WBS, rye, wheat, or popcorn (see corresponding TEK protocols)
- **Bulk substrate**: CVG (coco coir, vermiculite, gypsum) — see [CVG Substrate TEK](/protocols/general/CVG%20Substrate%20TEK/CVG-Substrate-TEK%5Bbulk-substrate%5D.md)
- Drill and 1/2" or 3/4" drill bit (for polyfill holes) — OR
- Soldering iron (for melting polyfill holes)
- Polyfill (polyester pillow stuffing) for filter patches
- Hot glue gun (to secure polyfill)
- Spray bottle with clean water
- 70% isopropyl alcohol
- Thermometer and optional hygrometer

### Optional Materials

- Fluorescent or LED grow light (6500K) on 12/12 timer
- Humidity gauge (hygrometer)
- Heating mat (for temperature control in cold environments)
- Perlite (for humidity layer under monotub, for passive humidity setups)

## Protocol

### Step 1: Monotub Preparation

**Trigger/Timing:** Before or concurrently with substrate/spawn preparation

#### Procedure:

**Standard Monotub Hole Configuration:**

A standard monotub setup uses passive or active FAE through polyfill-covered holes drilled in the sides of the tub.

1. Mark hole positions:
   - 4 holes on each long side wall, approximately 2-3 inches from the top edge
   - Holes should be positioned above the anticipated substrate level
   - Typical hole size: 1/2" to 3/4" diameter

2. Drill or melt holes:
   - Use drill with appropriate bit, or heat a metal rod/soldering iron to melt through plastic
   - Clean up any rough edges

3. Install polyfill filter patches:
   - Cut polyfill squares slightly larger than holes
   - Apply hot glue around hole perimeter (inside and outside of tub)
   - Press polyfill firmly over hole until glue dries
   - Polyfill allows gas exchange while filtering contamination

4. Clean and sanitize tub:
   - Wipe interior thoroughly with 70% isopropyl alcohol
   - Allow to evaporate fully before adding substrate

**Alternative: Shotgun-Style Monotub**

- Drill 1/4" holes every 2 inches across all sides, bottom, and lid
- Fill holes with polyfill and secure with hot glue or leave open for more aggressive FAE
- More holes = more FAE = lower humidity (requires more frequent misting)

**Quality Indicators:**

- Polyfill patches securely adhered with no gaps
- Interior clean and sanitized
- Holes positioned above anticipated substrate line

**Control Points:**

- Ensure polyfill is thick enough to filter contaminants (compress 1/2" thick layer minimum)
- Test seal by blowing through polyfill — should feel slight resistance

### Step 2: Spawning the Monotub

**Trigger/Timing:** When colonized grain spawn is fully consolidated and CVG substrate is prepared and cooled

**Reference:** See [CVG Substrate TEK](/protocols/general/CVG%20Substrate%20TEK/CVG-Substrate-TEK%5Bbulk-substrate%5D.md) for substrate preparation

#### Procedure:

**Spawn Rate:** 10-20% spawn to substrate by volume (higher spawn rate = faster colonization and less contamination)

**Layer Method (Recommended):**

1. Sanitize hands and work surface with isopropyl alcohol
2. Add 1-2 inch base layer of CVG substrate to monotub bottom
3. Break up grain spawn into individual kernels
4. Spread a layer of grain spawn evenly over substrate base (approximately half the total spawn volume)
5. Add 2-4 inches of CVG substrate over spawn layer
6. Spread remaining grain spawn over second substrate layer
7. Top with final thin layer of CVG (optional casing layer)
8. Level surface gently — do not compact

**Mix Method (Alternative):**

1. Combine spawn and substrate in large mixing bowl
2. Mix thoroughly to distribute spawn evenly
3. Transfer to monotub and level surface

**Total Substrate Depth:** Target 3-6 inches of substrate in the tub

**Quality Indicators:**

- Spawn distributed evenly throughout substrate
- Substrate at field capacity (not dripping wet)
- Surface leveled without compaction
- Tub not overfilled — minimum 4-6 inches of headspace above substrate

**Control Points:**

- Work quickly during spawning — minimize air exposure time
- Higher headspace in tub allows more humid microclimate to develop
- Tub should not be excessively large for the substrate amount (reduces headspace humidity)

### Step 3: Colonization

**Trigger/Timing:** After spawning — seal tub for colonization

#### Procedure:

1. Place tub lid on monotub:
   - Lid can be sealed completely during early colonization (first few days)
   - After day 3-5, leave lid slightly offset (1/2 inch) for minimal FAE
   - Polyfill holes provide passive FAE throughout colonization

2. Store in colonization environment:
   - Dark or low-light area
   - Stable temperature

3. Monitor daily through tub walls:
   - White mycelium should appear from spawn points within 5-10 days
   - Full surface colonization typically in 10-21 days

4. Do not open the tub during colonization unless you observe contamination

5. Colonization complete when:
   - White mycelium covers entire substrate surface
   - Mycelium has consolidated (dense, thick, slightly hardened surface)
   - Small pins may begin forming even before fruiting conditions are introduced

**Growth Parameters:**

- Temperature: 24-28°C (75-82°F) for cubensis; adjust per species
- Humidity: Maintained by closed tub (high CO₂ environment)
- CO₂: Elevated during colonization (beneficial — suppresses contamination)
- Light: None required during colonization
- Time: 10-21 days for full surface colonization

**Quality Indicators:**

- Dense, white, uniform mycelial mat across substrate surface
- No contamination (green, black, pink mold)
- Mycelium may show "overlay" (dense, skin-like layer) — see troubleshooting

**Control Points:**

- Maintain consistent temperature — avoid swings that cause condensation
- Do not introduce FAE prematurely — high CO₂ during colonization suppresses contamination
- Inspect from outside the tub to minimize contamination introduction

**Troubleshooting:**

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
| ------- | ----- | -------- |
| Green mold patches | Contamination; insufficient spawn density | Discard if widespread; small spots may be managed by removing |
| No growth after 2+ weeks | Non-viable spawn or temperature too low | Verify spawn; raise temperature to 26-28°C |
| Overlay (hard, leather-like surface) | Too much CO₂ or temperature too high | Introduce more FAE; scrape surface to expose substrate |
| Condensation on walls | Temperature swings | Improve temperature stability; ensure even heating |

### Step 4: Initiating Fruiting

**Trigger/Timing:** After full colonization and mycelial consolidation

#### Procedure:

1. Introduce fruiting conditions:
   - Begin regular FAE: open tub and fan 2-3 times daily (30-60 seconds each)
   - OR for passive FAE: offset lid 1-2 inches, or open polyfill holes fully
   - Mist interior walls of tub (NOT directly on substrate surface)
   - Reduce temperature 2-4°C from colonization temperature

2. Lighting:
   - Provide 12 hours on / 12 hours off cycle
   - Indirect natural light or fluorescent/LED grow light (6500K)
   - Light acts as a directional cue for pin formation

3. Pin development (primordia formation):
   - First pins typically appear 5-10 days after introducing fruiting conditions
   - Pins appear as tiny white dots on the substrate surface
   - Allow pins to develop — avoid disturbing tub during early pin development

4. Growing fruits:
   - Continue misting walls to maintain 80-95% relative humidity
   - Continue FAE 2-3 times daily
   - Caps should develop within 5-7 days of pin formation

**Growth Parameters:**

- Temperature: 20-24°C (68-75°F) for cubensis
- Humidity: 80-95% RH
- CO₂: < 1000 ppm (FAE critical for pin formation and cap development)
- Light: 12h on / 12h off (indirect)
- Time: 5-10 days to first pins, 5-7 additional days to harvest-ready

**Quality Indicators:**

- Multiple pin sites across substrate surface
- Pins developing into recognizable mushroom shapes with caps and stipes
- Caps not elongated (long spindly stems indicate CO₂ excess)
- Veils intact on developing fruits

**Control Points:**

- FAE is the single most critical factor for successful fruiting — insufficient FAE = no pins or elongated pins
- Mist walls, not pins — water directly on pins can cause blotching
- Harvest timing: just before or as veil begins to tear (prevents spore mass and improves yield)

**Troubleshooting:**

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
| ------- | ----- | -------- |
| No pins after 2+ weeks of fruiting conditions | Insufficient FAE, overlay, or wrong temperature | Increase FAE; scrape overlay; adjust temperature |
| Elongated, spindly stems | Excess CO₂ (insufficient FAE) | Increase FAE frequency and duration |
| Pins abort (stop developing) | Humidity drop, temperature swings, or disturbance | Stabilize conditions; increase misting |
| Contamination during fruiting | Introduced with FAE or misting | Improve FAE source air quality; avoid direct misting on substrate |

### Step 5: Harvest and Post-Flush

**Trigger/Timing:** Just before or as the partial veil begins to tear

#### Procedure:

1. Harvest:
   - Sanitize hands/gloves with isopropyl alcohol before harvest
   - Grip fruit body at base and twist gently while pulling up
   - Alternatively, cut at substrate level with clean scissors or knife
   - Collect all mushrooms including small pins

2. Clean up after harvest:
   - Remove all mushroom stumps and aborted pins
   - Stumps are primary contamination sites — remove completely
   - Wipe excess spores from tub walls

3. Rehydrate substrate between flushes:
   - **Field cap method**: Add 1-2 cups of clean room-temperature water to substrate surface; allow to absorb for 30 minutes; drain any standing water
   - **Dunk method**: Remove substrate block (if solid) and submerge in clean cold water for 12-24 hours; return to tub

4. Resume fruiting conditions:
   - Continue FAE and misting as in Step 4
   - Second flush typically appears 7-14 days after rehydration
   - Repeat for 3-5 flushes total (until substrate contaminates or is exhausted)

**Quality Indicators:**

- Clean harvest with no stubs or aborts left behind
- Substrate rehydrated to field capacity
- No widespread contamination after first flush

**Control Points:**

- Complete stub removal is critical for multi-flush success
- Contamination risk increases with each flush — monitor closely
- Substrate typically produces 3-5 flushes before exhaustion

## Optimizations

### Efficiency Improvements

- **Higher spawn rate (25-33%)**: Colonizes faster, more resistant to contamination, may increase first flush yield
- **Casing layer**: 1/2" layer of 100% field-capacity coco coir or 50/50 coir/verm on substrate top promotes uniform pinning
- **Cold shock trigger**: Move colonized monotub to refrigerator for 24 hours before introducing fruiting conditions — triggers aggressive pin set

### Scaling Considerations

- Monotubs scale from 6-quart for single-person use to 106-quart for high-volume production
- Multiple monotubs can be staggered (different colonization phases) for continuous harvest
- Monotub method is the most popular bulk fruiting technique on Shroomery for its simplicity and yield

### Resource Optimization

- Tubs are reusable across many cycles (clean with dilute bleach between uses)
- CVG substrate is inexpensive and widely available
- A single monotub can produce 100-500+ grams of fresh mushrooms per cycle

## Community Notes

- The monotub is the most popular bulk fruiting method on Shroomery for cubensis cultivation
- The key insight of monotub is maintaining a humid microclimate inside the tub while allowing sufficient FAE
- "Overlay" (a dense, skin-like mycelial mat that blocks pinning) is the most common issue; fix by scratching the surface and increasing FAE
- Many experienced growers prefer the 66-quart Sterilite tub as the ideal size/price balance
- Some growers use "unmodified" monotubs (no holes) and simply leave lids cracked — this works but is less consistent
- The spawn-to-substrate ratio debate is ongoing on Shroomery; 20% spawn rate is the community consensus starting point
